# Abstract (final, 243 words; same text as report/saboot-report.html)

Hindi newspapers report central government schemes in numbers, and readers, fact-checkers and AI research agents need to know whether those numbers agree with official statistics. Track 2 of this sprint asks what share of a domain's content can be linked to checkable evidence; we answer that question for one real domain. We collected 102 numerical claims about six central schemes from four Hindi news outlets (August to November 2026) and searched Press Information Bureau releases, scheme dashboards, archived snapshots and Parliament answers for a dated official figure for each. Of the claims, 45.1% (46/102, 95% CI 35.8-54.8%) could be bound to an official statistic: 24.5% cleanly, to the same metric, geography and period, and 20.6% only ambiguously, mostly because the nearest official figure was dated weeks or months away. Coverage was 69.2% for national claims but 20.0% for state-level claims. Among the 25 cleanly bound claims, 88.0% matched under strict rounding and 92.0% within a 5% band. Every official page behind a binding was opened in a browser, and every recorded evidence quote was found in the saved page text. Labels were produced with AI assistance under fixed written rules, without a second human labeller. We also release a ready-to-run protocol and prompt for testing whether LLM verifiers abstain or invent official sources; it has not been run. The main lesson is that coverage is limited by where official numbers are published, so a verifier for this domain must often answer "cannot verify".
